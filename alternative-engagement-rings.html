Alternative Engagement Rings

Designed around one stone

An alternative engagement ring is any ring that starts from what you actually love, not from a showroom case of near-identical diamond solitaires. It might be a Montana sapphire the color of a lake at dusk, a salt-and-pepper diamond with a galaxy inside it, or a fantasy-cut stone that catches light in a way a standard cut never will. At Bohemi, every ring is designed around its stone and made to be worn for a lifetime. Different is better, and it always has been.

Bohemi is a Boulder design studio for people who want the opposite of off-the-shelf. Each ring is designed by Heather and built around one stone. Because no two stones are alike, no two rings come out exactly the same, even when the design is one we have made before. You are not picking a finished ring off a shelf. You are choosing the stone it gets built around, and that is what makes it yours.

Stone first

What does it mean to design a ring around the stone?

The stone is the heart of the ring, so it comes first. We source and select the center stone with you, and the design follows it. In a bespoke piece that means drawing from scratch around the stone. In a ready-made or made-to-measure piece it means fitting an existing design to the stone you chose. That order matters either way. It is what lets the metal, the profile, and the setting serve the stone instead of fighting it, and it is why no two Bohemi rings come out exactly the same.

The honest answer

Is every Bohemi ring one of a kind?

The stone always is. The design sometimes is, and the honest answer depends on which ring you’re buying. Some Bohemi rings are true one-offs: made once, around a stone that can’t be found again, and when that ring sells, it’s gone. Others are designs Heather can build again, even though the stone in them never repeats. A lab diamond can be matched exactly. A particular Montana parti sapphire cannot.

Should you choose a lab diamond or a natural one?

Beyond these, we work in spinel, and in both lab and natural diamonds. A lab diamond, also called a synthetic diamond, is a real diamond, chemically and optically identical to a mined one, and usually many thousands less, which is why some couples choose it. Clients choose a synthetic to sidestep sourcing concerns, and some want a natural stone specifically. There is no single right answer, only the one that fits your budget and your values. We will talk it through honestly, including which softer stones we steer away from for a ring you will wear every day.

Will an alternative engagement ring hold up to daily wear?

Every Bohemi ring is designed to survive a real life. That is a standard, not a slogan. There is enough metal around every stone to hold it safely, never thinner than half a millimeter at the setting, because a ring you have to baby is not a ring you can actually live in. The trend of a giant stone on a wisp of a band photographs beautifully but does not hold up, so we do not make it. A substantial stone gets a setting substantial enough to balance and protect it.

Where it comes from

Where do the stones and metals come from?

Every Montana sapphire we set is natural, the only treatment ever used is heat, and we always disclose it. Our Montana sapphires can be traced to the source, to named grounds like Rock Creek, Yogo Gulch, Dry Cottonwood Creek and the Missouri River gravel bars. Because they are mined in the United States, they come from operations held to regulated labor and environmental standards, which is a real part of why couples who care where a stone comes from choose them.

Questions people ask first

What is an alternative engagement ring?
It is a ring built around what you love rather than a standard diamond solitaire. That usually means a distinctive center stone (a fantasy cut, a Montana sapphire, a salt-and-pepper diamond, a colored stone), a one-of-a-kind design, or both. At Bohemi, every ring is designed around its stone. A bespoke piece is designed for one person and never repeated.
Are lab diamonds real diamonds?
Yes. A lab diamond, also called a synthetic diamond, is chemically and optically identical to a mined diamond. The only difference is that one is grown in a lab and the other is pulled from the earth. It usually costs many thousands less, which is why some couples choose it. One honest note: lab diamonds have essentially no resale value, so buy one to wear and keep.
Are the inclusions in a salt-and-pepper diamond flaws?
No. Those black and white flecks are natural characteristics, and every stone’s pattern is one of a kind. A salt-and-pepper diamond is still a full-hardness diamond at a 10 on the Mohs scale, so it stands up to daily wear, and it lives outside the commodity white-diamond market, so you get more stone for your budget.
Does a fantasy cut sparkle more than a standard cut?
Not exactly, and that is the honest answer. A standard brilliant cut actually scintillates more. What a fantasy cut gives you is an even brightness that holds from many angles as your hand moves, a larger face for its carat weight, and a lower, more secure setting for everyday wear.
Where do your sapphires come from?
Our natural sapphires are only ever heat treated, with treatment always disclosed. Most carry mine-level traceability back to a specific Montana mine, which is rare in the industry. We also love sapphires from Australia for their teal-yellow color zoning, Madagascar’s Royal Blue, Tanzania’s vivid purples, and Nigeria’s unmatched saturated greens. We also carry a few lab sapphires, all in fantasy cuts.
Can I design something completely my own?
Yes, and you do not need the words for it. You bring the feeling and a few images, and we translate that into a real design built to last. The studio is by appointment, so your consultation time is entirely yours.
Can you use a stone I already own?
Yes. Resetting an heirloom stone into something new is part of what we do, alongside men’s pieces, wedding bands, and stacks built to sit together.
