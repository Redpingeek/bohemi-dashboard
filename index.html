The thinking behind it

The authority pages

When someone asks Google or an AI assistant for an alternative engagement ring, the recommendation goes to whoever owns that category. Right now that is not you, even though it is exactly what you do. The hub makes Bohemi the answer for the whole category, and the two deep-dive pages catch the specific searches your buyers already make, salt-and-pepper diamonds and Montana sapphires, that you were not yet showing up for. It puts the authority you already earned to work on the terms you own outright.

The stone matcher

Most of your buyers arrive overwhelmed, going off a feeling with no jewelry vocabulary. The quiz meets them right there, asking about color, light, and lifestyle instead of cut and clarity, and it hands you a decoded brief of what they are picturing. It does the first pass of the translation you are so good at, so the consultation starts warm instead of cold, for both of you.

Image accessibility, handled

What we found

We went through every image on your site. The good news: most already carry alt text, the short description a screen reader reads aloud and Google reads too, because you add it as you post, and you write it well. Only a set of images had that field left blank. Those are the ones we are closing.

Your product photos, done in one pass

For your product images we filled every blank description in a single bulk update, drawn straight from your catalog so each one is accurate: the piece, the metal, the form. Nothing for you to click through. This was the most exposed part, and it is closed.

Your blog images, now complete

Your blog had 104 images with that field left blank, across 21 posts. Every one of them is now handled. We wrote and applied 82 of them, matching the way you already describe your own work: the metal, the stone, the form. We looked at every single photo rather than guessing from its file name, because file names lie more often than you would think.

The remaining 22 could not be written, because those photos are missing from your site entirely. That is the one thing left, and it is below.

Your voice, kept

Your blog descriptions are richer than anything automated could write. "Champagne salt and pepper emerald cut diamond in 14k yellow gold inside a pink flower" is yours, and it should stay yours. So you keep writing them as you publish, the way you already do, and we simply catch the few that slip through so nothing sits empty.

The rhythm going forward

When you add an image, add a line describing it, which you already do naturally. On a regular schedule we scan the whole site and hand you a short list of anything left blank, with the exact spot to find it, so there is no hunting and no guessing which image is missing. Product photos we can refresh in bulk anytime from your catalog.

Your fail-safe, for the weeks you are slammed

Behind that scan is a tool that writes descriptions for you. For product photos it fills them straight from your catalog, in bulk, in plain accurate language, without anyone touching them one by one. For blog photos, where a real person needs to look at the picture, it drafts and we finish. Either way, if you get busy and cannot get to a batch, it does not sit empty and exposed. When you have the time, your words always win. It is the backup that keeps this from ever piling up again.

Measurement

The number that matters most: are AI assistants recommending you?

Measured July 17, 2026, by asking two AI assistants the same thirteen buyer questions twice each, which is fifty two answers in total. Bohemi came up in eight of them. We ask the exact same questions every time so the readings are comparable.

  • Where you already come up: someone asking for a custom ring in the Boulder or Denver area, and someone asking for boho or celestial style rings. Your witchy engagement rings post is doing that second one on its own.
  • Where you are absent: alternative center stones, salt-and-pepper diamonds, Montana sapphires, fantasy cuts, and ethical one-of-a-kind rings. Those five are exactly what the three new pages are built to win.

One honest limit: on the second assistant we cannot reliably detect when it quotes you, so a zero there means we cannot see it, not that it never happens.

Why this second table is here, and why it is second.

There are two separate doors people come through now. One is Google, where someone types a search and clicks a result. The other is an AI assistant, where someone asks for a recommendation, reads the answer, and comes straight to you. This table only describes the first door. Nothing that happens at the second door shows up in it.

We still watch it closely, because of what it predicts. The research on this is clear and your own site agrees with it: the pages Google places well are far more likely to be the ones an AI assistant quotes. Every page an assistant currently names for you sits in Google's top twenty five. The subjects where you have no presence in Google, salt-and-pepper diamonds most of all, get quoted by nobody. So this table is not a measure of your business. It is a measure of whether the door is open.

What a low number here does not mean. It does not mean nobody came. A visit that arrives because an AI assistant recommended you never appears in Google's numbers at all. Your rings also sell in person and by private invoice, so a page Google records almost no activity for can still be the reason someone walked through your door. We will never judge this work by clicks alone, and neither should you.

What we are watching next:

  • Your stone pages climbing toward the place on Google's results list that your collection pages already hold.
  • Your listings starting to show extra detail directly in Google's results, such as price and availability, which the new labeling makes possible.
  • AI assistants beginning to name you for Montana sapphire, salt-and-pepper diamond engagement rings, and fantasy cuts. The three new pages get their own tracking the day they go live.
