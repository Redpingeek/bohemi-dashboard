A stone with a real address

A Montana sapphire is a natural stone with a real address. It is mined in the United States, and most of ours can be traced to a specific mine, which is rare in this industry. It arrives in colors a diamond never will, from soft cornflower blue to teal to the shifting parti stones that hold more than one color at once. And at a 9 on the Mohs scale it is genuinely built for a ring you wear every day. For a lot of couples, this is where an alternative engagement ring begins.

At Bohemi, sapphire is one of our foundations, and every stone is chosen with you and designed around its color and character. This is a stone we know deeply, from where it comes out of the ground to which color will still feel like you in thirty years.

A real address

Where do Montana sapphires come from?

Most colored stones arrive with no specific history at all. Our Montana sapphires can be traced to the source, to named Montana grounds like Rock Creek, Yogo Gulch, Dry Cottonwood Creek, and the Missouri River gravel bars. Because they are mined in the United States, they come from operations held to regulated labor and environmental standards, which is a real part of why couples who care where a stone comes from choose them. Every Montana sapphire we set is natural, and the only treatment ever used is heat, which we always disclose. There is no undisclosed treatment and no mystery origin. When a stone is going to sit on your hand for a lifetime, knowing where it came from is part of what makes it yours.

Nature-made color

What colors do Montana sapphires come in?

Four stones from the current catalog. Every one is a different color, and none of them was chosen to make that point.

Montana is best known for Yogo Gulch and its cornflower blue, a true blue mined deep underground rather than sifted from riverbed gravel, from a mine that is now closed, which only makes that rough rarer. But teal is the color the state has become best loved for more recently: a blue-green that shifts with the light and often carries more than one hue in a single stone, which is what makes a parti sapphire feel truly nature-made and one of a kind. Dry Cottonwood Creek runs to teal, sage green, and blue. Rock Creek produces the widest, most prolific range of fancy colors of any deposit in the state; the stones come out of the ground in soft pastels, and respond beautifully to heat treatment, which draws out highly saturated purples and pinks. Missouri River is where the warm end of the palette lives: yellows, oranges, and soft peaches.

Are sapphires durable enough for an engagement ring?

Sapphire sits third in hardness, behind only diamond and moissanite, which makes it one of the few colored stones truly suited to daily wear. That is why it anchors so much of what we make. As with every Bohemi ring, the setting is designed to protect the stone and to last, with enough metal to hold it safely and a band substantial enough to balance it. A ring you can actually live in is the whole point.

How do you design a ring around a Montana sapphire?

Because the stone leads, we start there. We source and select your sapphire with you, guided by color, cut, and how it will wear, then design the entire ring around it. A teal parti stone wants a different setting than an icy Montana blue, and that decision is part of the craft. Questions people ask first

Are Montana sapphires treated?
Every Montana sapphire we set is natural, and the only treatment ever used is heat, which we always disclose. There is no undisclosed treatment.
Can I design a fully custom sapphire ring?
Yes. We source your sapphire with you, guided by color and how it will wear, then design the whole ring around it. You bring the feeling and a few images, and we translate that into a real design built to last.
What is a parti sapphire?
A parti sapphire holds more than one color in a single stone, most often a teal that shifts between blue and green. Because the coloring happens in the ground, no two are alike.
Will a teal sapphire look the same in daylight as it does indoors?
Not exactly, and that is part of the appeal: these stones shift with the light. It is why we take great care to show you what the stone looks like in daylight, shadow and incandescent lighting.
How long does a Montana sapphire engagement ring take to make?
It depends on how custom you go. A ready-made design takes about 4 to 6 weeks, made-to-measure about 6 to 8, and a fully bespoke piece about 8 to 12.
